What we do

We translate a technical industry into language that works — for policy, retail, investors, and the people who eat what you grow.

Seaborn Media is a creative studio built around one sector. We don't pretend to cover everything. We cover seafood, aquaculture, and the wider food system — with the depth that requires.

Science becomes content. Data becomes narrative. An industry that reads like a regulatory brief starts to read like something worth paying attention to.

The problem

Seafood has a communication problem.

01 — Data

The numbers exist. Yield, traceability, footprint, mortality. They sit in quarterly reports almost nobody opens.

02 — Science

The research is strong. It stays locked behind journals, acronyms, and 90-page PDFs that no buyer will ever read.

03 — Narrative

What the sector doesn't have is a voice. So consumers, media, and policy keep writing the story for it.

How we work

A method borrowed from editorial, not from advertising.

Most agencies arrive with a template. We arrive with a reading list. Seafood doesn't need louder campaigns — it needs clearer ones.

01

Read the science first.

Before we write a headline, we read your reports, your lab notes, your regulatory exposure. No shortcuts.

02

Find out who's actually listening.

A buyer at a Nordic retailer, a policy officer in Brussels, and a consumer in Madrid don't need the same sentence.

03

Write like the reader matters.

Short where it can be short. Technical where it has to be. Never padded to look important.

04

Design so nothing gets lost.

Typography, hierarchy, layout. The format is part of the message — especially when the message is complex.

Why niche matters

Niche, on purpose.

Most agencies take any brief. We take the ones we understand. That changes what lands on the page.

We don't start by googling your species. We already know the difference between RAS and open net, between MSC and ASC, between fishery management and fisheries policy.

That's the whole point.

Content style

A point of view on how this sector should sound.

Editorial, not promotional.

If it reads like marketing, it has already lost the readers who matter most — the informed ones.

Researched, not recycled.

No borrowed talking points. Every piece starts with primary sources and the people actually doing the work.

Built for insiders first.

If it passes the technical audience, it will pass the rest. The other direction doesn't work.
